# Static in the Wire

**Fandom:** Original Cyberpunk | **Rating:** T | **Pairing:** Rin/Cass | **Tags:** slow burn, enemies to allies, forced proximity

*Two rival couriers are locked in the same transit station for one night while a storm grounds every flight.*

## 1. Stormfront

Rin shifted her weight from one foot to the other, her grip tightening on the weathered cardboard box cradled in her arms. The package was small, too small to be significant in a world driven by weighty cargo, but it was everything to her—an irrefutable proof of her reliability. The station’s fluorescent lights flickered above, casting an uneasy glow that mirrored her growing frustration. Even through the persistent din of the crowd, a low rumble echoed from outside as the storm began to unleash its fury.

“Come on, it’s just a little rain. What, you afraid of getting your hair wet?” Cass’s voice, smooth and glib, cut through the murmur of commuters. He lounged against the wall, his dark hair tousled, a crooked smile playing on his lips. He made it look effortless as he flipped open a weathered book, the cover creased and faded. 

“Very mature, Cass,” Rin replied, her tone clipped as she tried to ignore the warmth creeping into her cheeks. She needed to stay alert, to focus on the job at hand, especially with Cass nearby. “Some of us actually have deadlines to meet.”

“Right, deadlines. Worth dying for, I’m sure.” He turned another page, his humor unyielding even as the wind howled like a banshee outside. 

The air between them crackled, something unspoken hanging over it like rain clouds—a tension born from too many encounters, too many rival deliveries gone awry. She locked her gaze on the box, calculating the distance to her destination, each inch a small victory she could snatch from the chaos brewing outside.

“Who knew you could care so much about a single delivery?” Cass’s tone dripped with mockery, but there was an edge to it, a familiarity that both unnerved and intrigued her. He tilted his head, eyes glinting as if he could see right through her tough exterior.

Rin shot him a sidelong glance, her expression as guarded as a fortress. “Maybe you should care a little more about your work instead of spending all your time cracking jokes.” She hated how easily he could provoke her, how he somehow slipped past the walls she’d built. 

“Look, I’m just trying to lighten the mood.” The book snapped shut, his hands braced against the wall as he stood up. “You’d think with all the tech out there, we wouldn’t be stuck in a glorified waiting room during a downpour.”

The announcement system crackled to life, a harsh reminder that they were on borrowed time. “Attention, all travelers. Due to severe weather conditions, all flights are grounded until further notice.” 

A wave of frustration flooded through her, boiling just beneath the surface. “Great. Just perfect.” She set the box down for a moment, rubbing at the tension in her neck, and shot a glare at Cass, who looked far too entertained by her predicament. “I’m supposed to get this across town. I don’t have time for this.”

“Hey, I’m as trapped as you are,” he replied, his voice closer to sincerity, yet still laced with that signature glibness. “No one likes a delayed courier, especially not me. I was on my way to get some shut-eye. I bet I could sleep right here, and still deliver better service than you.”

“Don’t count on it,” Rin retorted, rolling her eyes. “You’ll just end up dreaming about losing. Again.”

“Not if I can help it.” He leaned closer, the teasing glint returning to his eyes. “I’m more of a ‘forget about the competition and wake up in a new galaxy’ type, you know?”

Rin crossed her arms, dislodging the fidgeting habit she’d slipped into. She’d always prided herself on her control, on her ability to keep the focus where it mattered. “You think this is a joke? This package is important.”

“And you think you could break a world record with it?” A shadow danced across his expression, something deeper swirling beneath his nonchalant facade. 

“I’m not here to impress you, Cass.” Her tone was sharper, the resolve burning just under the surface while she glanced around the station, her mind racing to figure out how to salvage the night. The storm outside was relentless, the howling winds like a cautionary tale, whispering that procrastination was an enemy. 

A flicker of uncertainty crossed his face, gone in an instant as he shoved his hands into the pockets of his worn jacket. “Yeah, I get that. But we both know it’s not as simple as just delivering anymore, right?” His gaze pinned her, cutting through the bravado.

This was not a conversation she wanted to have, especially not now, not here. “What’s your point, Cass?”

“Just… don’t be too hard on yourself. We’re all a little lost sometimes.” 

Rin narrowed her eyes, unwilling to let him worm his way past her defenses, especially not in this half-lit purgatory where shadows had a way of deepening and twisting. “What do you know about being lost?” 

The reply hung in the air, charged, unfinished. Outside, the storm screamed louder, shaking the glass doors as if it too sought entry. As she met Cass’s gaze, the tension continued to weave between them, shifting, threatening to change the stakes. 

“Let’s hope we don’t have to find out,” he said, a smirk dancing at the corner of his mouth. 

They were both stuck there, together, and somewhere inside Rin, a small ember of conflict began to ignite, flickering against the encroaching darkness.

## 2. Static Connections

Cass leaned back against the cool wall of the transit station lounge, letting the weight of exhaustion settle heavy in his bones. The glow of flickering overhead lights created a stuttering rhythm in the otherwise dull atmosphere, mirroring the chaos twitching just below the surface of his mind. He could hear Rin fidgeting not far away, her shoes tapping a quick pattern on the floor, and he fought the urge to glance her way. 

“Don’t even think about it,” he muttered to himself, trying to drown out the sound. He closed his eyes and breathed, focusing on the faint hum of the generator in the distance, the periodic crackle of the lights. 

Yet the sensation that something was about to shift hung thick in the air. The screen on the communal terminal chimed, jolting him from his reverie. Casually, he opened his eyes, half-expecting another notification about the increasingly chaotic weather—but instead, Tess’s face filled the screen, bright and bubbly despite the chaos outside. 

“Cass! Rin!” Her enthusiasm cut through the drudgery of the station. 

Cass nearly groaned as Rin turned, her gaze sharpening. “What the hell is she doing here?” 

“Not my doing,” he said, waving his hand dismissively though his heart quickened at the unexpected intrusion. “Tess thought she was calling one of the couriers, not both.” 

“Oh, candid camera, is it? Beautiful,” Rin shot back, the corners of her mouth twitching ever so slightly. 

“Maybe she just wants to remind us how much better her life is,” Cass replied, maneuvering the conversation with a practiced glibness despite his body's desire for stillness. 

Tess continued her stream of chatter, oblivious to the tension rippling between them. “So, you guys stuck there too? Crazy storm, huh? Heard they grounded all flights! It’s a nightmare!” 

“About your ten minutes too late,” Cass replied, rolling his eyes even as the laughter of an audience played in his mind, a memory of laughter mingled with camaraderie that felt painfully out of reach right now. 

Rin studied his face, something unspoken flickering between them. “Is that all it is to you, Cass? Just a nightmare?” 

Her tone made it challenging to maintain his mask. “Better than a dream, right? Nothing worse than waking up to a world gone to hell.” 

She leaned closer, the curiosity in her eyes cutting past his bravado. “Is that what you tell yourself? That it’s all just a bad dream?” 

Cass opened his mouth to retort but paused, the retort caught under the weight of understanding that tangled in her gaze. Something shifted, a crack in the facade that had built up between them like the barriers of a city skyline. 

Tess, unaware of their burgeoning tension, continued. “Anyway, I’m glad you both are safe! Can’t believe you’re together, though! Seriously, why don’t you just, like, collaborate? You know you’re in the same mess, right?” 

The thought of collaboration twisted in Cass’s mind—he could almost hear the familiar beat of a shared rhythm, a connection that shattered all the past rivalries that had built their walls. “Right. Because putting our heads together will probably bring down the whole station,” he snarked, leaning away again. 

“Oh come on, it wouldn’t kill you to try,” Tess’s persistence trailed off as the lights flickered again, a rapid strobe that made Cass’s heart stutter in his chest. 

Rin’s posture tightened, and Cass could sense a shift through the tension thickening the air. “What do you know about it?” she challenged Tess directly. “You don’t know anything.” 

Before Tess could respond, the lights blinked out entirely. In an instant, the station was engulfed in darkness, the usual hum and chatter strangled into silence. 

Cass’s heart raced; awareness heightened now, everything felt sharp, encased in velvet blackness, pressing around them like claustrophobia. “Great,” he muttered, glancing around, marking the shapes of shadows without any real sense of direction. “Just what we needed—a blackout.” 

“Just don’t trip over your own feet,” Rin’s voice emerged first, a steady, anchored tone amidst the chaos. “It’s not like you could pass for a professional at that either.” 

“That’s rich coming from you,” Cass shot back, though he could feel the uncertainty creeping into the edges of his own bravado. 

“Are you scared?” Rin’s challenge hung softly in the air, the dark pressing against them, stripping away the exteriors they usually wore. 

“Who’s scared?” Cass retorted, but his voice came out far more strained than he intended. 

As the silence deepened, he could sense the hesitation where their rivalry might have stood. Unseen, he shifted closer, feeling the heat radiating off her in the darkness, fragile, threads of vulnerability weaving between them. “It’s just dark,” he finally admitted, a chuckle escaping him, uncertain in its weight. 

“Keep telling yourself that,” Rin said, and Cass couldn’t help the half-smile that followed. 

Outside, the storm raged on. Inside, two couriers found themselves teetering on the precipice of the unknown, buried beneath layers of rivalry, fatigue, and the quieter truths begging to surface, a silent storm brewing between them.

## 3. Fractured Frequencies

A dull thud echoed through the dimly lit station as Rin's palm met the metal bench, a hollow clang accompanying her simmering frustration. Her other hand clenched the strap of her courier bag like a lifeline. "It's not just a missing package, Cass. It's my reputation on the line."

Cass, slouched against the wall with an infuriatingly casual stance, shrugged, the light from a sputtering emergency fixture casting jagged shadows across his features. "So they say," he replied, voice dripping with sarcasm. "Packages go missing all the time. It's part of the game."

"A game?" Rin snapped, her voice cutting through the stagnant air like a blade. "You might think this is just some trivial thing, but I took on this contract in good faith."

"Right, and I'm here for the air conditioning," Cass shot back, rolling his eyes. "Face it, we live on shifting sands—one minute you’re up, the next you're down."

Their words crashed into each other, overlapping like the relentless pelting of the rain outside. But beneath the sharp exchange was another layer—an unspoken acknowledgment of how much they depended on being right, on winning, on surviving in this ruthless dance.

Then, as if on cue, the storm redoubled its fury, the howling wind shaking the station to its core. A different silence settled after—a pause crackling with static potential. Rin sighed, relinquishing the anger that clung to her like a second skin. "We're getting nowhere like this."

Cass's expression shifted, his bravado momentarily yielding to a flicker of exhaustion. "Truce?" he offered, a hesitant olive branch extended with a rueful half-smile.

Rin regarded him, searching for sincerity beneath his ever-present mask. Finding a glimmer of it, she gave a curt nod. "Fine. A truce."

Together, they huddled at a table littered with outdated schedules and discarded coffee cups, a tactile map of their purgatory. Cass pushed aside debris and spread out a crumpled flight chart. "Look, if the storms clear, the first flights will be international. We need a tight plan."

They leaned in, their heads close enough for her to catch the faint scent of Cass's rain-damp hair, which smelled of engine oil and something indefinably human. "We'll need contingencies," she said, running scenarios through her mind.

Gradually, their heated exchanges cooled into something more focused, a shared cadence in their working rhythm. They bounced ideas off each other, words weaving an impromptu tapestry of strategy and survival. For once, the object of their competitiveness shifted from each other to the collective task before them.

Time stretched into unfamiliar shapes, marked only by the distant roll of thunder and low hum of emergency lights. Between plans and calculations, conversation wove itself into quieter places, seeking shelter from the storm.

Amid a lull, Cass spoke, surprising her with the softness of his voice. "Why'd you even get into this mess of a job, Rin?"

She hesitated, fingers tracing the edge of the table in mute contemplation. "Same reason as anyone, I guess. Because I've got something to prove."

He nodded, expression shadowed by something—understanding, perhaps. "Can’t sleep without the noise," he admitted, casting his eyes briefly toward the station's high beams, where rain pounded with relentless symmetry.

"What are you running from?" she asked, the question gentle, without the sharpness of before.

Cass gave a small, hollow laugh. "Nothing that wouldn't bore you."

But there was no dismissing the truth that lingered in the spaces between his words, just as neither could ignore the underlying draw that tugged them ever closer, despite—or perhaps because of—everything else.

Their plans solidified into a coherent strategy, a scaffold of intent. The storm refused to let up outside, but inside, they’d found an unexpected clarity.

Rin leaned back, letting the tension in her shoulders unwind, only to realize the humor in their odd alliance. "If anyone asks, we were at each other's throats the entire time," she said, a half-smile grazing her lips.

Cass chuckled, the sound a low ripple through the static. "Wouldn't dare ruin your reputation."

A silence settled over them, not empty but full in its own way, as if words were no longer necessary. As they waited for dawn, for the chance to step back into the whirlwind of their lives, Rin couldn't shake the feeling of being on the edge of something—something that refused to be hidden beneath layers of rivalry and rain.

The night stretched on, but some part of her already knew they’d remember this—these fractured frequencies, the night a pivot in their otherwise steady orbit.

## 4. Clear Signals

Cass rubbed the sleep from his eyes, blinking against the pale, washed-out glow of the transit station lights. The world outside still roared with fury, but the storm had become a distant thrumming, a comforting backdrop rather than a terror. He turned to face Rin, still tucked beneath her makeshift blanket—an unremarkable jacket—her gaze focused on a far-off spot he couldn't quite place. It was as if she were hunting for answers hidden in the shadows.

“Guess the storm's not as chaotic as your sense of humor, huh?” he said, slipping back into familiar banter, though his tone was softer, testing the waters after their earlier moment. The corner of her mouth quirked upward, but it was fleeting; her defenses hadn’t crumbled entirely.

“Maybe.” She turned, meeting his gaze for a heartbeat before dropping it. “But it definitely makes me question my life choices.”

His eyebrows shot up. “Oh? Now I’ve got to know, when did you choose to be a courier?” The words had a playful bite, masking the seriousness he knew lay beneath.

“Underestimating my own stubbornness, I suppose.” She leaned back against the wall, folding her arms as if guarding herself against the chill. “Or maybe it was a moment of pride. I thought I could make a name for myself.” The vulnerability in her voice set him back, but curiosity inched him forward. “Turns out no one wants the package that got lost in transit. That’s where I excel—failure.”

“Guess we’re running a similar race then,” Cass said slowly. “I thought I was living the dream, you know? All glitz and glamour—until I realized it’s just drudgery wrapped in neon lights.” He gestured, chin raised, about the metal walls, their reflections flickering in the light. “I used to think I could outrun everyone, get out before I got swallowed up. Now?” He paused, letting the weight hang for just a moment. “I barely manage to keep my shift schedule in check. I don’t even know why I’m still doing it.”

Rin shifted, a small sigh escaping her lips. “It’s common ground, then. The things you do to prove yourself—driving against the tide.”

For a moment, silence enveloped them, and Cass felt the tension shift, the rivalry morphing into something unspoken but real—a fragile understanding. “Yeah, but at the end of the night? It’s just the same unsealed envelope, isn’t it? Might look different on the outside, but we’re both just fighting for the next delivery on the schedule.”

“Exactly.” She smiled at that, the edges of her mouth pulling up into a soft curve. “I guess it’s nice to know I’m not the only one guessing in the dark.” 

“We’re either destined for greatness or a corner booth at the nearest bar,” Cass shot back, half-sarcasm and half-serious. “At this point, I’ll take a good drink over the endless cycle of paperwork any day.”

“I don’t drink,” Rin said, unfaltering, but he caught a flicker of amusement in her eyes.

“Right, right. Too busy being all responsible.” He leaned forward, tempted to nudge her lightly. “How about this? Once we get out of here, I’ll introduce you to the concept of a night out. Maybe I’ll allow it to be a ‘responsible’ drink. Think of it as—research.”

“Research, huh?” she echoed, her brows raised, an incredulous note in her voice.

“Exactly. We can dissect the merits of reckless choices and questionable cocktails.” 

“I think I just got a little nauseous,” she replied, mirth filling her tone. “But maybe I’ll get the full experience… just to understand why people tolerate it.” 

They exchanged glances, a tether forming across the space between them, each realizing that their rivalry, once fiery, was morphing into something more inviting, something filled with strange warmth. 

“Have you ever thought about what happens when the storm clears?” Cass asked, leaning back against the wall beside her, suddenly aware that dawn wasn’t as far away as it once seemed.

“What do you mean?” She shifted her weight, focusing fully on him.

“I mean, when the flights resume, and we’re back out in the city? We’ll be right back where we started—racing each other for the next delivery. But what if we didn’t have to go back to that?” The words tumbled forth before he had the chance to catch himself.

Her brow furrowed in thought, lips pursing slightly as if the very notion surprised her. “You’re suggesting we team up?”

“Not suggesting, just… throwing out ideas. You said it yourself—chaos breeds chaos. But what if we exploited that instead? Fewer deliveries for both of us.” 

The blush of dawn glimmered timidly through the storm-shrouded windows, a hint of light creeping inside. “There’s something in that, isn’t there? A partnership, even?” Her voice struck a note of intrigue with a cautious undertone.

“What if we let the storm clear, and then decide?” Cass offered, heart racing with the implications. “Maybe we could be more than just rivals.”

Rin met his gaze, eyes glinting with something he couldn’t yet name. “Let’s see how the dawn looks first.” 

They hovered there, poised on the edge of something uncharted, understanding hovering like a whisper, waiting to be acknowledged.

The air was heavy with the scent of rain, almost metallic, as they stood in the dim light filtering through the glass. Cass sensed the moment hanging between them, fragile yet electric, like the tension before a storm. He shifted his weight, an uncharacteristic hesitation creeping into his demeanor. “Never thought I’d be considering a partnership with you of all people,” he said, a half-smile curving his lips, though the weight of the moment tugged at the corners.

“And I never thought I’d actually contemplate, well…” Rin trailed off, the words flitting past her lips as she considered the dawn seeping through the grime. “I guess it’s ridiculous.” She quickly masked the thought with a self-deprecating laugh, but there was an ember of sincerity in her eyes that caught him off guard.

“Ridiculous can be good sometimes,” he countered, nudging the boundary between banter and something deeper. “Especially in this line of work.” He leaned closer, the thrill of danger still crackling in the air — not the faint hum of high-stakes delivery, but the risk of something unknown.

She narrowed her eyes, contemplating, a smile just beginning to play at her lips. “You’re alright, Cass. Just don’t let it get to your head,” she retorted, injecting a hint of her typical bravado into the air. 

As silence stretched between them again, Cass caught a glimpse of the light shifting and found himself instinctively reaching out. Their fingers brushed, a fleeting moment that didn’t just spark electricity; it ignited something unspoken in the dimness around them. A width of understanding pressed against the tension of the moment, aching yet exhilarating. 

Rin’s gaze dropped to their hands, caught in an unguarded place. Slowly, she interlaced her fingers with his, a small, defiant gesture that shattered the silence, binding them together in this unexpected clarity, the storm brewing outside forgotten, if only for now.
